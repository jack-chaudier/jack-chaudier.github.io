Project Rhun

Toward Pylos‑1

A research program about what a language model is entitled to say, and a decoder built to treat context as custody. No trained model exists yet. This page is the honest shape of the work.

Status: no trained model · training unrun · record private

The goal

Pylos-1 is the decoder I am working toward: one that treats context as custody and lets loss be visible. Not a LoRA wrapper, not a scanner, not a product UI.

Project Rhun is the unified research record behind it: the inventories, proofs, experiments, and the trail of what was tried, kept in one place under rules that stop the vocabulary from drifting. It folds in everything I had been doing under the Mirage name.

The question the program asks

The test

Does the finite mirage shelf persist under unbounded archive and learned decoder?

In small settings that can be checked exactly, there is a shelf: a stretch where a model's answers stay correct after its entitlement to them has already fallen away, because the evidence was evicted, revoked, or superseded and nothing in the decoder noticed.

The program asks whether that shelf survives when the archive is unbounded and the decoder is learned rather than hand-built. If it does not, the job is to name the object that replaces it. Either answer would be a result. Neither is in hand.

Three ideas

  1. The mirage

    A correct answer the model was not entitled to. Entitlement is not correctness. A model that says the right thing after the witness for it was lost is not right; it is lucky, and it will be lucky in the wrong direction next time. The program counts the correct unentitled answer as the failure, not the success. The gap between accuracy and justification, the share of answers that are right without being earned, is the quantity the whole thing is organized around.

  2. Context as custody

    Context is not a transformer window. A kernel compiler takes the history and the query and decides, for every retained byte, whether it is resident, a capsule, pageable, or opaque; each byte is exactly one of those. The model proposes; it never authorizes. What it can cite is what it was actually given custody of, and a citation to something it never read is rejected before anything is released.

  3. Visible loss

    Every budget is finite, so something is always left out. The rule is that whatever the budget cannot hold is written to a ledger with an address, so it can be paged back in, or the answer can say unknown, and here is where it went. Nothing is dropped silently. A compaction that loses a distinction has to leave a mark where the distinction was.

The program

The record is organized by area. Each area has its own definitions, and the metrics are deliberately kept apart: three different lines each define a justified rate of their own, and they are never compared with one another.

  1. pylos-1

    The destination. It holds the training proposal (Contract-Quotient Training: train a context state to preserve the distinctions that future continuations such as audits, revisions, counterfactuals, and requests for grounds will need, not just the current answer) and CLAIM-0, a preregistration of the first measurement: a small untuned model inside a verifier, with the kill rules written down and frozen before the run. The proposal is unrun. The measurement is not a gate, and it has not completed.

  2. mirage-geometry

    The small exact settings where the shelf can be computed and the accuracy-versus-justification gap measured directly, with the refutations filed next to the memos.

  3. justification-gap

    What a model can say versus what it can show, and how the two come apart once the witness is gone.

  4. semantic-reason

    An earlier line on reasoning over meaning. Part of the record; not the kernel object.

  5. dreams

    The papers (paper I and papers 00–03), archived on Zenodo as 10.5281/zenodo.18793829 and 10.5281/zenodo.18794293.

  6. protected-witness-audit

    Brute-force checks over protected witnesses at small parameters. A finite check, not an infinite proof.

  7. revelation-context

    Runs on naturally occurring mirages rather than synthetic ones.

  8. stark

    Memos on witness-faithful factorization. Stark on its own does not prove the leap to unbounded archives or to language models.

Status

Where things actually stand

No trained Pylos-1 exists, and training has not been run. The training proposal is a proposal. The preregistered measurement is frozen with its kill rules and has not completed.

Nothing in the program is an established result about learned models, and I am not putting any benchmark number on this page. What exists is the record: definitions that do not drift, a test that can fail, and the code and evidence needed to run it.

Where it lives

  1. Pylos, the shipped side

    Pylos is open source (pylos.vercel.app): “the forever chat”, a kernel and product built for the regime the research describes, with a 1,000,000-turn hash-chained bench. Pylos-1 is the research goal of a decoder trained for that regime.

  2. The papers

    The dreams papers are public on Zenodo: 10.5281/zenodo.18793829 and 10.5281/zenodo.18794293.

  3. The record

    The program record itself is a private research record, and I keep it that way on purpose. Ask me about it.

Before Rhun

The Mirage-era work, MirageKit and mirage-mcp (a repo-local memory runtime for coding agents), mirage-geometry, and miragebench, was the first pass at the same question under a different name. Rhun folds it in rather than replacing it: the useful objects were kept, the metrics were separated so they stop being confused with each other, and the naming stopped moving.
